Shirley Erena Murray (b. Invercargill, New Zealand, 1931)

studied music as an undergraduate but received a master’s degree (with honors) in classics and French from Otago University. Her upbringing was Methodist, but she became a Presbyterian when she married the Reverend John Stewart Murray, who was a moderator of the Presbyterian Church of New Zealand. Shirley began her career as a teacher of languages, but she became more active in Amnesty International, and for eight years she served the Labor Party Research Unit of Parliament. Her involvement in these organizations has enriched her writing of hymns, which address human rights, women’s concerns, justice, peace, the integrity of creation, and the unity of the church. Many of her hymns have been performed in CCA and WCC assemblies. In recognition for her service as a writer of hymns, the New Zealand government honored her as a Member of the New Zealand Order of Merit on the Queen’s birthday on 3 June 2001. Through Hope Publishing House, Murray has published three collections of her hymns: In Every Corner Sing (eighty-four hymns, 1992), Everyday in Your Spirit (forty-one hymns, 1996), and Faith Makes the Song (fifty hymns, 2002). The New Zealand Hymnbook Trust, for which she worked for a long time, has also published many of her texts (cf. back cover, Faith Makes the Song). In 2009, Otaga University conferred on her an honorary doctorate in literature for her contribution to the art of hymn writing.

雪莉·埃琳娜·默里（Shirley Erena Murray） MNZM （née 公鸡，1931年3月31日至2020年1月25日）是新西兰的赞美诗词作者。她的赞美诗已被翻译成多种语言，并在140多个赞美诗集中有代表。

出生在 因弗卡吉尔 并提出了一个卫理公会，[1] 她受过教育 南国女子高中,[2] 并获得了 艺术大师 荣誉学位 经典 和来自 奥塔哥大学.[3] 她后来担任老师和研究员。[4]

结婚后 长老会 1954年，约翰·默里（John Murray）部长[4] 她最终移居到惠灵顿，约翰在1975年至1993年期间担任圣安德鲁教堂的部长。她的赞美诗写作始于1970年代，经常将圣安德鲁教堂用作圣诗集的测试场所。[1] 许多不同的作曲家都在她的赞美诗中加入了音乐。[1]

她的赞美诗已被翻译成多种欧洲和亚洲语言，并在全球140余首赞美诗中都有代表。[3] 除新西兰外，它们还特别用于北美。[1]

在她最著名的赞美诗中，有“安扎克节赞美诗”，“开阔天空的山峰”，“我们的生活有四季”，“明星儿童”和“颠倒的圣诞节”。[1][3]

教授兼赞美诗人科林·吉布森（Colin Gibson）为她的某些歌曲设定了音乐，[1] 穆雷（Murray）在2009年的赞美诗中说：“因其包容性语言和对毛利人的创新使用，对世俗用语的大胆挪用以及从自然和家庭生活中汲取的原始诗意形象而区别开来，但同样也因他们面对当代问题的直接性而与众不同。”[3]

在里面 2001年女王生日荣誉，默里被任命为 新西兰功绩勋章成员，作为赞美诗作者的服务。[5] 在2006年，她成为了 皇家教会音乐学院。她于2009年获得奥塔哥大学（University of Otago）的名誉文学博士学位。同年，她被任命为奥塔哥大学（University of Otago）的研究员。 美国和加拿大的赞美诗协会.[3]

默里与丈夫住在一起 劳马蒂海滩 在惠灵顿附近。这对夫妇有三个孩子和六个孙子。[6] 她于2020年1月25日去世。[7]

Shirley Erena Murray MNZM (née Cockroft; 31 March 1931 – 25 January 2020) was a New Zealand hymn lyrics writer. Her hymns have been translated into numerous languages and are represented in more than 140 hymn collections.

Biography[edit]

Born in Invercargill and raised a Methodist,[1] she was educated at Southland Girls' High School,[2] and earned a Master of Arts degree with honours in classics and French from the University of Otago.[3] She later worked as a teacher and researcher.[4]

After marrying Presbyterian minister John Murray in 1954,[4] she eventually moved to Wellington where John was minister for the St Andrew's on the Terrace from 1975 to 1993. Her hymn writing started in the 1970s and often used the congregation of St Andrew's as a testing place for the hymns.[1] Many different composers have put music to her hymn texts.[1]

Her hymns have been translated into several European and Asian languages and are represented in more than 140 hymn books around the world.[3] In addition to New Zealand, they are particularly used in North America.[1]

Among her most known hymns are "Hymn for Anzac Day", "Where Mountains Rise to Open Skies", "Our life has its Seasons", "Star Child" and "Upside Down Christmas".[1][3]

Professor and hymn writer Colin Gibson, who has set music to some of her songs,[1] described Murray's hymns in 2009 as "distinguished by their inclusive language and their innovative use of Māori, their bold appropriation of secular terms and their original poetic imagery drawn from nature and domestic life, but equally by the directness with which they confront contemporary issues."[3]

In the 2001 Queen's Birthday Honours, Murray was appointed a Member of the New Zealand Order of Merit, for services as a hymn writer.[5] In 2006, she became a fellow of the Royal School of Church Music. She received an honorary doctor of literature degree from the University of Otago in 2009. The same year, she was named a fellow of the Hymn Society in the United States and Canada.[3]

Murray lived with her husband at Raumati Beach near Wellington. The couple had three children and six grandchildren.[6] She died on 25 January 2020.[7]
