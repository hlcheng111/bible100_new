　【恭請主來】
詩集：世紀頌讚，167

1 Jesus, come! for we invite you,
guest and master, friend and Lord;
now, as once at Cana's wedding,
speak, and let us hear your word:
lead us through our need or doubting,
hope be born and joy restored.

2 Jesus, come! transform our pleasures,
guide us into paths unknown;
bring your gifts, command your servants,
let us trust in you alone:
though your hand may work in secret,
all shall see what you have done.

3 Jesus, come in new creation,
heaven brought near in power divine;
give your unexpected glory,
changing water into wine:
rouse the faith of your disciples--
come, our first and greatest Sign!

4 Jesus, come! surprise our dullness,
make us willing to receive
more than we can yet imagine,
all the best you have to give:
let us find your hidden riches,
taste your love, believe and live! 

耶穌，我們恭請你來作我主、嘉賓、良朋；
正像昔日迦拿婚筵，聆聽聖言彰權能：
領我經過疑慮需要，重獲希望喜樂增。

耶穌，使我們得快樂，領我前行願跟隨；
帶來恩賜，吩咐僕人，全心信靠勿違背：
雖然聖手暗中運行，世人必見你作為。

耶穌，來到新造世界，大能頒天福眷佑；
彰顯出人意外榮耀，將清水變為美酒：
激發門徒滿有信心，初次偉大神蹟留。

耶穌，來！使愚鈍驚奇，願意接受你恩典
超過我們所想所望，一切美好來自天：
願意尋找隱密豐富，飽嘗主愛信更堅。

Christopher Martin Idle (b. Bromley, Kent, England, 1938)

was educated at Elthan College, St. Peter's College, Oxford, and Clifton Theological College in Bristol, and was ordained in the Church of England. He served churches in Barrow-in-­Furness, Cumbria; London; and Oakley, Suffolk; and recently returned to London, where he is involved in various hymnal projects. A prolific author of articles on the Christian's public responsibilities, Idle has also published The Lion Book of Favorite Hymns (1980) and at least one hundred of his own hymns and biblical paraphrases. Some of his texts first appeared in hymnals published by the Jubilate Group, with which he is associated. He was also editor of Anglican Praise (1987). In 1998 Hope Publishing released Light Upon the River, a collection of 279 of his psalm and hymn texts, along with suggested tunes, scripture references, and commentary.

Bert Polman

Notes

SICILIAN MARINERS is traditionally used for the Roman Catholic Marian hymn "O Sanctissima." According to tradition, Sicilian seamen ended each day on their ships by singing this hymn in unison. The tune probably traveled from Italy to Germany to England, where The European Magazine and London Review first published it in 1792. The tune was associated with the German Christmas carol "O du Frohliche, O du Selige." The tune also appears to have had an influence on the African American song “We Shall Overcome.”

SICILIAN MARINERS is a bar-form tune (AAB) with a florid soprano line and an active harmonization. Sing it either vigorously or reflectively; singing rather deliberately will increase the hymn's dignity. A traditional version of the tune includes dotted rhythms, which encourage a more meditative approach to singing.

--Psalter Hymnal Handbook, 1988

Christopher Martin Idle (born 11 September 1938) is a British hymnodist.

Idle was born in Bromley, Kent, and studied at Eltham College, St Peter's College, Oxford, and Clifton Theological College, Bristol.[1] He was ordained as a priest in the Church of England in 1965, and is a member of Reform.[1]

Idle has written more than three hundred hymns.[1] "Eternal light, shine in my heart" has been included in The Hymnal 1982, Together in Song, and Voices United,[2] while "God, we praise you! God we bless you!" is in Christian Worship, Evangelical Lutheran Hymnary, and Together in Song.[3] Other hymns by Idle include "This earth belongs to God", "Spirit of holiness", "When lawless men succeed", "Freedom and life are ours", "Then I saw a new heaven and earth", and "As sons of the day and daughters of light".

Idle has been part of what has been described as a British "hymn explosion" since World War II.[4]

人生路上與復活主的相遇  (請耶穌來)

經文【可五22–43】

講員：梁明財牧師 記錄：杜玉蓮姊妹

今天我想和大家分享馬可福音第五章裏一個熟識的人物故事：睚魯，他來到耶穌面前，請求耶穌到他家中。聖經講述耶穌到睚魯家路上所發生的事情。

我們都是耶穌復活之後認識我們的信仰，但在睚魯的時代，人們對復活主沒有認識。拉撒路復活之前耶穌同他的姐姐馬大說：「我就是復活，就是生命。信我的人，雖然死了，仍然要活著。」(約十一25，《現代中文譯本》)復活和生命都在於主耶穌，由主所掌管。所以睚魯可以在人生路上與復活主相遇；馬大、馬利亞、拉撒路都一樣經歷與復活的主相遇。

一、人與主耶穌的互動

今早崇拜我們所唱的尊崇詩歌是《恭請主來》，聖經記述睚魯「請」耶穌到自己家裏，同樣在我們信仰的開端亦經歷過「請」耶穌來進入我們的生命裏。但在這段經文裏我們又發現相反句：「耶穌，請你不要來」(可五35，六1-6)這可能同樣在我們信耶穌以前，有人曾向我們傳福音，我們拒絕聽；或是有人邀請我們去教會，我們會拒絕去。拒絕聲如高呼：「耶穌，請你不要來」。在(可五35)睚魯的家人知道睚魯的女兒死了，就說不須耶穌來了。而同樣在(可一38-39)我們又看到耶穌不請自來。耶穌很願意走遍各城各鄉，不須請耶穌都會前來。馬可福音第一章記載：主的門徒希望祂回到受歡迎的城市與群眾再聚，但耶穌告訴門徒祂不是為了「受歡迎」及「掌聲」而來，祂要去其他有需要的地方。所以耶穌是不請自來，只要有需要的地方祂都會去。

1.請耶穌來(可五22-24)

第一個請耶穌來的是睚魯，睚魯對耶穌有信靠的信心。其實這樣的信心對睚魯並不容易，因為他當時的身分是管會堂的人，社會地位很高，是猶太人會堂裡的領袖，屬有權、有勢、有財的人。但他願意來到耶穌面前請求耶穌，沒有理會別人用麼眼光來看他作的這件事。睚魯知道耶穌有能力醫治女兒，所以就來到耶穌面前請耶穌到家裏醫治女兒。當睚魯的女兒死訊傳來，耶穌對睚魯說:不要怕，只要信甚！其實，害怕不單是睚魯會有的經歷，有時候我們也會害怕一些事情。這種害怕是與我們信甚麼、依靠甚麼有關係。如果信靠耶穌，睚魯與我們都會曉得要害怕、敬畏耶穌，要聽耶穌的吩咐。但有時候我們卻會害怕另外一些東西，這就是信其他東西多過信靠耶穌了。耶穌知道睚魯的軟弱，所以給他鼓勵:不用怕，只要信。睚魯請求耶穌就是信靠，這信靠是要不斷做的，不是某一個時刻才做。耶穌安慰睚魯只要信靠就可以成就了，這就是睚魯得到的恩典。

2.耶穌，請你不要來(可五35，六1-6)

另一個我們會經歷的，是「耶穌，請你不要來」。人們都是傾向用自己的眼光或方法去看事物。當睚魯的家人看見睚魯的女兒死了，就通知睚魯不用勞煩耶穌來救治——「耶穌，不用來了!」當人看見女孩死了就覺得絕望。這是人的看法，以為死亡就是一切的完結。但耶穌告訴他們從神的角度來看：死不是絕望，是可以看得見盼望。所以，當耶穌到達睚魯的家，就將那班哀哭的人趕走。耶穌拉着小女孩的手，叫她:「起來！」耶穌有自己的方法，而人會傾向人的想法。雖然睚魯表達對耶穌的信靠，但在這信靠中他還是用人的眼光、方法去做事。他邀請耶穌到家中按手醫治女兒，按手治病是當時文士和拉比會用的方式。睚魯認為耶穌也要用按手的方法治病，但耶穌不一定要用這個方法。

在耶穌往睚魯家的路上，發生了一件事情：有一個患了12年血漏的婦人摸了耶穌的衣服一下，她的病就馬上得到醫治。耶穌停下來找尋摸他衣服的婦人，因此耽誤了去睚魯家的時間。本來睚魯很想耶穌盡快到家裏治病，但因為患血漏婦人的出現，耽誤了耶穌去睚魯家的時間。耶穌去到睚魯家時，女孩已死去。人的想法不同耶穌的想法，人的想法是：「耶穌遲到了！」，但我很想告訴大家，這件事情如果發生在人身上，遲到會是大事；但發生在耶穌身上，則完全不會影響救治，因為時間掌握在耶穌手中。人會以為耶穌遲到，女孩就失救死了，但不明白耶穌可以遲到，因為耶穌不受時間的限制，他任何時候都可以醫治。普通的醫生就不能遲到了。所以，很多時候人用自己的方式、想法去作判斷。

3.耶穌不請自來(可一38-39)

睚魯邀請耶穌是用邀約的方式，但在我們人生路上不用邀約耶穌，也會「偶遇」耶穌。不請自來，表示耶穌很主動按着計劃來參與我們的人生。耶穌主動介入人的生命，人的反應會如何呢？會否害怕耶穌來到？所以有時候會想：「耶穌，祢最好不要來了！」當耶穌去睚魯家之前，發生了格拉森事件：耶穌在格拉森遇上一個被一群污鬼附着的人，他將污鬼全部從那人身上趕出，因此犧牲了兩千頭豬（可五1-20）。結果，當地人就請耶穌離開，哀求耶穌不要再來了。在人眼裡所看見的，就是兩千頭豬的價值比鬼附的人得到康復更為重要，但耶穌所看重的，是人的生命，耶穌與人的看法截然不同。在很多事上人不明白耶穌所作的，例如：五餅二魚的神蹟(可六38-44)，還有耶穌在水面行走，門徒就以為是鬼怪，極其害怕。(可六49-50)正因為人的理解有限，故此對耶穌的教訓與所做的事都未能完全明白。

耶穌有祂的時間表。剛才少年詩班所獻唱的詩歌，歌詞表達了神有最完美的計劃，神的智慧高過人的想像。耶穌沒有時間的限制，不會因為救患血漏的婦人而影響救睚魯的女兒。此外，可七章記載有一外邦婦人來到耶穌面前，求耶穌醫治女兒，遭到耶穌的為難，我們可能會覺得耶穌為甚麼要為難那個女人呢？其實，因為耶穌在祂的救贖計劃裏有祂的時間表。在徒一8耶穌給門徒一個應許：聖靈將會降臨在他們的身上，他們要出去為神傳揚福音，從耶路撒冷、猶太全地、撒瑪利亞，直到地極，這是神救恩的先後次序。(約二4)在迦拿婚宴上耶穌告訴母親：祂的時候還沒到；同樣在（約七6）耶穌也告訴兄弟他的時候還沒到。

二、抓緊與主相遇的機會

1.人在何時與主相遇？

人同耶穌的互動提醒了我，也提醒了你，你和耶穌的互動是哪一種呢？是請耶穌來的那種人，還是高喊「耶穌，請你不要來！」那種人呢？我們真的要抓緊與這位復活主相遇的機會。那位患了12年血漏婦人就抓緊了與主相遇的機會，得到醫治平安回家。其實我們也都曾經抓緊與主相遇的機會，就是決志信主的時候。抓緊機會成為耶穌的門徒，這是每一個基督徒都經歷過。我們都有不同與主相遇的經歷，例如在患病時，或在苦難中，還有其他時機都可以是與主相遇的機會。但是，人們面對苦難當前反倒逃避，因而離開了主。若在你身邊有弟兄姊妹正處苦難中，希望這些苦難都能成為你與主相遇的機會。亦有些時候，是因為參加教會舉辦的社關活動，成為人們得到與主相遇的機會；教會十字架的記號能讓人想起基督教信仰，吸引人到主面前來，也喚醒了一些正遠離神的人，重新回到神身邊。代表神的記號也能成為人的幫助和祝福。昔日約櫃也是猶太人視為神同在的記號(撒上四章)，認為約櫃代表神，有神同在就能打勝仗。

參加教會聚會也能遇見神，五月份教會舉行佈道會，就是幫助人與神相遇的時刻。還有我們的見證亦能幫助人與主相遇。我想分享一次醫院佈道的經歷，導師說醫院佈道工作很重要，因為人一般都不想來教會，但大部分人一生中都會有兩次機會在醫院裏：第一次，是出生的時候，第二次就是死亡或臨近死亡的時刻。所以導師就鼓勵我們把握機會到醫院作關懷探訪的佈道事奉，引領人與耶穌基督相遇。特別是面對死亡的人，更要捉緊機會讓他們與主相會。

2. 走在人生路上的「機遇」

現代人很喜歡講「機遇」，機遇可聯想為：有不同地方就有不同的發展機會，但有否想過最好的機遇是甚麼呢？。路加記載了三段的「人生路上機遇」：第一個是耶利哥的路上，第二個是以馬忤斯的路上，第三個是掃羅走在大馬色的路上。而在人生路上，最重要是與主相遇。以馬忤斯與大馬色的路上，耶穌都是不請自來，分別與兩位門徒及掃羅同行。當這兩位門徒和掃羅願意謙卑的時候，就明白到原來遇見到主。耶穌主動與人相遇，但人未必會察覺到、認識到，因為人沒有謙卑的心來認識主。（路廿四13-35）以馬忤斯路上的另一個提醒是：人自以為熟識聖經。從以馬忤斯路上的兩個門徒讓我們知道，我們對聖經的認識很少，我們應該多讀聖經，認識神的話。(徒九1-19)掃羅在大馬色路上的經歷讓我們看到：掃羅自以為能看準時機，結果神讓保羅瞎了眼，那時他才醒悟到，過去他用人的眼光看事情，以致做了很多與神對抗的事。後來保羅在(腓三1-6)講述已將自己過往一切視如糞土，大馬色路上與主相遇是掃羅人生的轉捩點。

人生路上我們有不同的經歷，有時候會在十字路口找出路，甚至走進「掘頭路」。耶穌都很想與我們相遇，我們不要等到走進人生的盡頭才與主相遇。「掘頭路」英文翻譯成Dead end ,又被翻譯成死路、死胡同。是否真的要走到人生的盡頭才找到出路呢？不是的，我們要把握機會。Dead 不一定是End ,死不一定是完結，復活主會給一條出路予我們。我們不要走到盡頭才醒悟原來主從始至終都與我們一起。復活節紀念復活的主，聖誕節給我們更大的提醒，就是耶穌是以馬內利的神！我們要珍惜以馬內利——神與我們同在、同行。今期的教會刊物「城內城外」主題是：與神同行，提醒我們有一位神，祂每時每刻都與我們一起，我們有否珍惜，切勿只是偶然與主相遇。我們要請耶穌做我們人生路上的「司機」，我們要完全信靠敬畏神，一切都在神手裏，讓神掌管，這樣我們就有盼望。弟兄姊妹們：請耶穌成為你人生路上的司機！讓祂帶領你走不同的人生路。

Bible Study  Jesus, come! for we invite you by Christopher Idle

Read: John chapter 2 vv 1-11

Background to the event
As well as its series of ‘I AM’ sayings, John’s Gospel is famous for his selection of ‘signs’; supernatural events which point to Jesus’ divine nature as ‘the Word’, the title introducing him in ch.1. Sometimes in reading a book, old or new, we wonder why the author wrote it. This one leaves us in no doubt, but tells in 23:30-31. John could have selected many other signs; he chose a few decisive ones to persuade his readers to believe that Jesus is the Son of God, and by believing, to have life in his name.

Other questions may arise from John 2:18 and 23:26 and 30. We limit ourselves to one story, in context. The signs are central to John’s aim – and this one (he carefully tells us) is the first. Unlike some Gospel events, the scene is familiar to everyone; a wedding. But although many of us have stories of wedding disasters, not many have seen one on the scale of v.3.!

Back ground to the hymn
The hymn was written in 1979 when I was Rector of Limehouse, for the Epiphany season when ‘the revelation of Christ’ is a central theme (the Greek word ‘epiphaneia’ means ‘appearing) and John 2 is read. The event is often called ‘the wedding at Cana’ but not many wedding stories leave the bride out altogether! As often happens, I was looking for a hymn to follow my sermon; several refer to the event in passing, but hardly any make it the main subject. So I started to write something new. For me, this short and unique story always holds fresh challenge and hope.

This hymn was published in a ‘Grove’ book by Robin Leaver in 1980, and later in several hymn books. It has been set to various tunes, and at least eight musicians have composed new ones. I owe much to them, and as ever, to friends whose comments on earlier drafts led to small but telling improvements.

Studying the Bible and the hymn
Whether studying alone, with someone else, or in a group, we need both the Scripture and the hymn in front of us. Although here we work through the hymn stanzas in order we must remember which comes first! The hymn aims to illustrate the Scripture without straying from John’s purpose and priorities. It should take us back to the bible and forward with it, not away from it. Hymns are ‘servants of God’s word’. There may be more questions here than you need or have time for; or you may want to ask your own. That is fine; these are only suggestions.

The plan of the hymn
The first two words of the hymn ‘Jesus, come!’ are repeated as the start of every stanza. Each time they lead into further prayers in a series of three couplets in every verse. The final two lines are meant to reach a climax of our petition to the Son of God who is revealed here. If these opening words seem a little stark (even rude!) they are meant to be heard or sung in the light of the second line, echoing another Biblical heart-cry: ‘Come, Lord Jesus!’ (Rev 21:20)

Stanza 1
Line 1: In what sense can we ‘invite’ Jesus? (Isn’t he the one who calls? – John 1.43 etc)

L.2 consists of four titles given to Jesus; how are the relevant to the events of John 2?

L.3 Some hymns use a singe place name (Eden, Emmaus, Nazareth) to set the scene. Here the name ‘Cana’ is used in this way. (Background: see also John 4:46 and 54:21-2. Cana is now known almost entirely for one thing; we could say, just for a few private family moments.)

L.4 ‘speak…’ Jesus’ few words in these verses include a question, a statement and two commands.
How might we have responded to each of these? Does he speak to us in similar ways?

L.5 Where is the need of doubting at Cana? A first draft of the hymn included the word ‘failure’, which many people feel keenly; should I have kept it in?

L6 The word ‘joy’ isn’t in John 2, but is seems a fair description. Does John 16:20 help here?

Stanza 2
Which verses of John 2 are suggested by this verse of the hymn?

Why the secrecy? How many people knew what had happened?

How does this make it the opposite of a mere conjuring trick? Can we think of times when Jesus has blessed people who do not even know where the blessing came from? Does it matter if they never do?

Stanza 3
‘New creation’ (line 1): if you are studying in a group, three different voices could read 2 Corinthians 5:17, Galatians 6:15, and Revelation 21:1 and 5. What do such texts have in common?

s Why do we need a new creation; what is wrong with the old?

‘Unexpected glory’; has this ever been our experience? Where else has Jesus ‘revealed his glory’?

‘Rouse the faith’; see John 2:11. What difference did this make to their understanding or commitment? (Some at least have begun to believe, in chapter 1) What factors have helped us to believe in Jesus?

In what sense is Jesus a Sign (line 6)? Let someone remind the group of Luke 2:12.

Why do you think this miracle, unlike many, happened only once?

Stanza 4, and the hymn as a whole
What does this stanza add to the hymn?

When was our dullness last surprised by Jesus?

If Mary were here to sing it with us, are there any lines she might specially identify with?

Is there anything significant in the Scripture which is missing in the Hymn? (What about John 2:4?)

The final line: even if you read this earlier, remind yourself, or one another, of John 20:30-31.

Can you suggest a better title (for the hymn and the event) than ‘the wedding at Cana’?

Has this study helped you to say what a miracle is?

When might be a good occasion for singing the hymn?

Two final thoughts
(a) The Book of Common Prayer speaks of ‘Holy Matrimony…which holy estate Christ adorned and beautified with his presence, and first miracle that he wrought, in Cana of Galilee.’ Newer liturgies mention the wedding but not the miracle. What might John think of that?

(b) John’s Gospel is described as ‘the book of signs’ followed by ‘the book of glory’. How do this event and this hymn reflect both parts?

At this point it may be possible to sing the hymn – when you have decided which tune fits best!

Bible Studies
One of the ways in which hymns can enrich our Christian lives is by becoming the springboard for bible study. This is doubly enriching in that it enlivens our study of the biblical text, and fills out our understanding of the hymn when we come to sing it. In inviting contributions for this selection of studies we have concentrated on contemporary material, and for a number, we have asked authors to provide a biblical study of one of their own hymns.
