簡介    God Is So Good
The exact origins of this chorus celebrating God's providential care are not clear, but it seems to have arisen during the 1970s. Its simplicity invites improvising additional text.
Because the text changes so little, the slowly rising four-note pattern of the tune provides the primary source of energy within its ﬁve-note range.

God Is So Good

Hymn Story/Background

Like many simple songs, the origins of this short chorus is unknown.  Its simplicity invites improvising additional text, and the fact that it has been translated into many languages testifies to its wide reception as a child-like creed and testimony.
— Emily Brink

Further Reflections on Confessions and Statements of Faith References

The Christology of the song, “God Is So Good” is carefully based on the content of the Apostles’ Creed, and the firmness of the convictions here echo the words of Belgic Confession, Article 20, that God gave “his Son to die, by a most perfect love, and [raised] him to life for our justification, in order that by him we might have immortality and eternal life.” “When [his] benefits are made ours, they are more than enough to absolve us of our sins…(Additionally,) the Holy Spirit kindles in our hearts a true faith that embraces Jesus Christ, with all his merits, and makes him its own, and no longer looks for anything apart from him” (Belgic Confession, Article 22).

"GOD IS SO GOOD"
"For the Lord is good; His mercy is everlasting…" (Ps. 100.5)

     INTRO.:

A song which reminds us that the Lord is good and His mercy is everlasting is "God Is So Good" (#47 in Hymns for Worship Revised). The 1992 Handbook to the Baptist Hymnal says, "No information is currently available concerning the origin of this anonymous song or its tune, God Is So Good." Most older books simply say "Traditional" or "Unknown." However, according to newer books, the text is believed to be a traditional American folk hymn by an anonymous author, and the tune is thought to be a traditional African folk song melody by an unknown composer. One website says, "Words and Music by Bobby Burnett, Videt Polk; Copyright 1958, renewed 1986 by Stamps Quartet Music Company." However, I suspect that this refers to a specific arrangement rather than to the song itself. There is another popular gospel song with the same title by Kevin Prosch which begins, "He rides upon the winds of the west, He is exalted by His name," and has a chorus, "God is so good, so good, so good, God is so good."

     In my collection of hymnbooks, the oldest one in which I can find the song is the 1976 New Church Hymnal edited by Ralph Carmichael for Lexicon Music Inc. It is also included with various arrangements in the 1979 Praise: Our Songs and Hymns edited by Norman Johnson for Singspiration Music; the 1987 Worship His Majesty edited by Fred Bock for the Gaither Music Co.; the 1989 Worship the Lord published by Warner Press; the 1991 Baptist Hymnal edited by Wesley Forbis for Convention Press; the 1993 Sing to the Lord Hymnal edited by Ken Bible for the Lillenas Publishing Co.; the 1997 Majesty Hymns edited by Frank Garlock for Majesty Music Inc.; the 1997 Celebration Hymnal edited by Jack W. Hayford for Word Music and Integrity Music; the 1999 Songs and Hymns of Revival edited by Jack Trieber for North Valley Publications; and the 2006 Christian Life Hymnal edited by Eric Wyse for Hendrickson Publishers Inc.

     Among hymnbooks published by members of the Lord’s church, the song may be found in the 1977 Special Sacred Selections (arranged by the editor) edited by Ellis J. Crum; the 1984 Rejoice and Sing (arranged by the editor) edited by Reid Lancaster; the 1986 Songs of Praise (words only), the 1990 Songs of the Church 21st C. Ed. (arranged by Reid Lancaster), and the 1994 Songs of Faith and Praise all edited by Alton H. Howard; and the 1992 Praise for the Lord edited by John P. Wiegand; in addition to Hymns for Worship. Since the song tends to be extremely repetitive, saying the same words three or four times in each stanza, I have taken the liberty of providing the following text, with words drawn from various arrangements plus some thoughts of my own; it requires a lengthened arrangement of the tune.

     The song encourages us to praise God for His goodness.

I. Stanza 1 says that God is good because He answers prayer
"God is so good, Shed Jesus’ blood, Saves from the flood, He’s so good to me!
He answers prayer, Takes every care, Gives me my share, He’s so good to me!"


 A. God had the blood of Jesus shed on the cross that He might save us and give us access to Him: Eph. 2.14-18
 B. Those who have taken advantage of this blessing have the assurance that God will hear and answer their prayers: 1 Jn. 5.14-15
 C. Thus, we can go to Him in prayer and know that we can cast ever care upon Him: 1 Pet. 5.7

II. Stanza 2 says that God is good because He cares for us
"Jesus is real, My soul will heal, My heart will seal, He’s so good to me!
He cares for me, He sets me free, His child I’ll be, He’s so good to me!"


 A. He cares enough to heal our souls from sin’s diseases: Mal. 4.2
 B. He cares enough to set us free from the bondage of sin: Rom. 6.17-18
 C. He cares enough for us to make it possible for us to be His children: 1 Jn. 3.1-3

III. Stanza 3 says that God is good because He saves us from sin
"He saved my soul, He makes me whole, He takes control, He’s so good to me!
I love Him so, His will I’ll know, To Him I’ll go, He’s so good to me!"
 A. God sent Jesus to save people from their sins: Matt. 1.21
 B. Therefore, He can make us whole so that we might be washed, sanctified, and justified before Him: 1 Cor. 6.11
 C. But to receive this great blessing, we must go to Him in response to His invitation to come: Matt. 11.28-30

IV. Stanza 4 says that God is good because He gave us His word
"He’s all I need, His words I’ll heed, He wants to lead, He’s so good to me!
I’ll do His will, His word fulfill, Obey Him still, He’s so good to me!"


 A. We need to heed His words because we shall be judged by them: Jn. 12.48
 B. Hence, it should always be our attitude that His will, not ours, be done: Matt. 26.39
 C. The reason that we must obey Him is to meet the conditions to receive eternal salvation: Heb. 4.8-9

V. Stanza 5 says that God is good because He will send Jesus to take us home
"I praise His name, His love proclaim, And sing His fame, He’s so good to me!
He’ll come again, To raise all men, I’ll see Him then, He’s so good to me!"


 A. As long as we live on earth we should be praising His name, proclaiming His love, and singing His fame to a lost and dying world that they might know of His salvation: Mk. 16.15-16
 B. We do this knowing that someday He will come again: Acts 1.9-11
 C. When that happens, we shall be raised from the dead and rise with all other righteous to meet the Lord in the air and ever be with Him: 1 Thess. 4.16-17

     CONCL.:

 As frequent readers of these hymn studies may know, I am not a big fan of the so-called "praise songs" (often called "camp songs") that are so currently popular, primarily because of their extreme repetitiveness. Some repetition is good, and even helpful to the memory, but to sing the same words over and over and over does little to teach and admonish and seems to border on "vain repetitions." Of course, how much repetition is going to have to remain a matter of judgment, and not everyone necessarily shares my judgment. However, I do think that some of the more popular "praise songs" may well have some merit and could be useful with adjustments. My version of this one may not be perfect, but I would like to think that it is a little improvement over the standard versions. In any event, it is needful that we be reminded at all times that "God Is So Good."
