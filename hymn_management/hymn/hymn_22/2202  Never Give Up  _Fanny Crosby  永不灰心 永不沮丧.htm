圣诗鉴赏《永不灰心 永不沮丧》丨Never Give Up

 圣诗推荐 2021-09-01
 
作者及诗歌介绍

这首诗歌选自“圣徒诗歌”456首，作者是我们所熟悉的芬尼?克罗斯贝(Fanny.Crosby,1820-1915)，是许多人所敬佩的诗人，享有“圣诗皇后”美誉。她创作的诗歌，多源于自身宝贵而真实的经历，首首动人心弦，如：《安稳在耶稣手中》、《有福的确据》、《救世主凡事引导我》、《他藏我灵》等。

芬尼出生于贫穷家庭，刚6个月就因病而被庸医弄盲了眼。她一生因主耶稣的爱，从不埋怨，也不灰心，那喜乐的人生，就象一颗闪亮的星、一股温暖的流，带给人们光明与亲善；她的诗歌更帮助、安慰、鼓励了千千万万个软弱忧伤的灵魂……

《永不灰心(Never Give Up)》，就是她的心声。从小具有音乐和文学天赋的芬尼，渴望进入盲人学校，但昂贵的学费远远超出她所能负担的。她曾求告主耶稣，但等了很久，神都没有成就这个心愿。她不灰心，也不丧胆，依然专心依靠主。结果，爱她的天父藉意想不到的方法，达成了她的愿望！她在学校念了12年，还留校当了11年老师，成为许多学生的榜样。正如圣经所说：“谁能使我们与基督的爱隔绝呢？难道是患难么？是困苦么？是逼迫么？是饥饿么？是赤身露体么？是危险么？是刀剑么？……然而靠着爱我们的主，在这一切的事上，已经得胜有余了”（罗8:35-37节）。

芬尼的祖母是一位非常爱主的姊妹，并且也相当的认识主。当她祖母抱她在膝盖上时，不仅把四围神创造的美丽，讲给她听，更把神的大爱、主的救赎和圣经的奇妙，灌输在她的心里。所以芬尼在很小的时候就有了重生的经历，且喜欢和主有亲密的交通。她深深的被救主十架舍命的大爱所感动。

这个爱火在她里面，越来越大；或许和她的眼瞎有关，因为她不能和外面的环境接触，所以她爱主的心是那样的单纯、真挚和热切。她看不见别的事物，只有救主藉着圣灵，将她自己的美丽，启示在她心里，因她整天用这样长的时间，在幔内和主交通，所以她从主直接所得着的认识和启示，不是一些从人得教导者所能比拟的。


她的祖母也把圣经读给她听，所以她从小就记得许多很长的经文。她祖母说：“孩子，你纵然不能像我一样，从书案上取出圣经来读，可是你能将许多经节——神的话，丰丰富富的珍藏在心里。以后你无论到哪里去，这些神的话都要随着你，比一切财物更宝贵，要成为你用不尽的宝藏。”

由于这种鼓励，她更加用心背诵圣经。后来她能背诵许多的诗篇，全本箴言和路得记，以及大部分的新约圣经。至于旧约圣经的故事，她也大多能背诵。她所下的工夫，对她以后一生的事奉，有深刻的影响，这些宝贵的圣经，成了她一生享用不尽的财富。论到圣经，她曾写下这样的诗句：

　　我所尊敬的书，你给我多少喜乐和安慰，　　我童年的良伴阿，你对我比宝石还尊贵。

芬妮享年九十四岁，死于一九一五年二月十二日。曾嫁给一位盲眼的音乐家Cana Alstyne，所以她的全名是Mrs.Fanny J.Crosby Alstyne。她的墓志铭上写着：“她所作的是尽她所能的。”

此诗歌作曲者爱伦山奇( Ira Allan Sankey)，是被称为近代“福音之父”Ira D. Sankey的儿子，芬尼非常喜欢他谱的曲。《永不灰心》的旋律、节奏、和声，与诗词极其吻合。整首歌以8分音符的轻快节奏进行，副歌突然改变节奏，音调提高，突出了诗歌的主题：“永不灰心”（2次）9-12小节；“专心靠主”（2次）17-20小节。第二次（19-20小节）音域向上，20小节出现了一个D和弦，新鲜又特别，好象一个大透气，然后音域推向最高点：“试炼中仍歌唱不住，刚强壮胆信靠主”

诗歌赏析

第一节：谁能“永不灰心，永不沮丧”呢？原来专心依靠主的人能够做到，因为荣耀的主是我们的盼望，只要将我们的难处向祂倾诉，根据主给的应许，必定赐给我们够用的恩典。

第二节：在生活中，因为各种各样的压力，使得我们思虑烦忧、愁眉苦脸……；转眼仰望主吧！祂是我们脚前的灯，路上的光，在祂恩光照耀之下，我们可以勇往直前，并且越走越光明，直奔祂荣耀的国度。

第三节：有基督生命内住的我们，理当“永不灰心，永不沮丧”，眼前的黑夜只不过是黎明前片刻之黑暗，等到主如晨曦显露之时，一切的阴霾都将成为过去，在祂荣耀的光中我们必定见光，并且要与永光的父永远同居。

第四节：有主成为我们的依靠和保护，在人生的道路上，即使遇到一点点难处，也不必灰心也不必气馁，更不必为自己讲理。要相信，主往往把“大爱”藏在患难里，只能希奇主给我们的是何等地深恩，我们一面感恩一面等候，直至主来的那日,得主的称赞和奖赏。


副歌：

在基督里永远不必灰心，永远不会因为从世界上来的琐碎之事而灰心丧气。要知道黑夜已深，白昼将近，光明之日即将来临。应当专心仰望主，单单依靠祂，即使在试炼中也当靠主刚强壮胆，不停地来赞美歌颂我们的主。

“NEVER GIVE UP” hymnstudiesblog


“Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience” (Jas. 1.3)

INTRO.:

A song which encourages us to have patience in the trying of our faith is “Never Give Up.” The text was written by Frances Jane Crosby VanAlstyne, better known by her professional name of Fanny J. Crosby (1820-1915). The tune was composed by Ira Allan Sankey, who was born on Aug. 30, 1874, in Edinburgh, Scotland. the son of Ira David Sankey, the well-known gospel song musician and singing director for revival evangelist Dwight L. Moody. After studying civil engineering and architecture at Princeton University, Allan graduated in 1897 and the following year joined the Biglow and Main music publishing company of New York City, NY, of which his father was President. Also, he served as vice-president of the Leeds and Catlin Phonograph Co. and served on the Advisory Board of the Greenich Bank. This song was published in 1903 but little background information is available about it. Among the hymnbooks that he edited or co-edited are The Male Quartette with George C. Stebbins, Best Endeavor Hymns with John Clements, Hallowed Hymns New and Old, and Standard Hymns and Spiritual Songs with Hubert P. Main.

Cyberhymnal credits Sankey with four other tunes besides this one: “Go and Work,” “Kept for Jesus,” “Lord, Send Us Forth,” and “A Year of Precious Blessings.” The Mennonite hymnbook Zion’s Praises edited in 1987 by Aaron Z. Weaver of Weaver Publishing Co. in Pittsgrove, NJ, has a hymn “Show Me Thy Way” with words arranged by Miss Crosby and tune by I. Allan Sankey. In 1908, Allan succeeded upon his father’s death as President of Biglow and Main, continuing until his own death on December 30, 1915, aboard the steam ship Korona. Among hymnbooks published by members of the Lord’s church during the twentieth century for use in churches of Christ, the only one in which I have ever seen “Never Give Up” is the 1965 Great Christian Hymnal No. 2 edited by Tillit S. Teddlie. However, among other books in my collection, it has appeared in the 1964 Service Hymnal published by the Hope Publishing Co., the 1968 American Service Hymnal published by John T. Benson, and the 1972 Living Hymns edited by Alfred B. Smith for Encore Publications.

The song suggests several things that will help us to persevere for the Lord in our lives.

I. Stanza 1 mentions faith

“Never be sad or desponding
If thou hast faith to believe;
Grace, for the duties before thee,
Ask of thy God and receive.”

There is never any reason to be sad or desponding when we are walking by faith: 2 Cor. 5.7
Those who have faith know that God will give them grace: Jas. 4.6
All they have to do is to come boldly before the throne of Christ and ask for grace to help in time of need: Heb. 4.16

II. Stanza 2 mentions prayer

“What if thy burdens oppress thee,
What though thy life may be drear,
Look on the side that is brightest;
Pray, and thy path will be clear.”

Sometimes we face burdens that oppress us: Gal. 6.2
However, we can look on the side that is brightest by casting our burdens on the Lord: Ps. 55.22
This we do by praying, making our requests known to Him, and He has promised to give us peace: Phil. 4.6-7

III. Stanza 3 mentions the hope of rest in the Lord

“Never be sad or desponding,
There is a morrow for thee;
Soon thou shalt dwell in its brightness,
There with the Lord thou shalt be.”

The “morrow” referred to here is the day when those who die in the Lord shall rest from their labors: Rev. 14.13
When our souls lay their burdens down, they can dwell in the brightness of the Lord: 2 Cor. 5.6-8
Then, we can go and be with the Lord: Phil. 1.23

IV. Stanza 4 mentions the Lord’s mercy

“Never be sad or desponding,
Lean on the arm of thy Lord;
Dwell in the depths of His mercy,
Thou shalt receive thy reward.”

Our merciful Lord opens His arms wide for us to lean on: Deut. 33.27
Therefore, as we live here and look forward to being with Him, we can look to His mercy: Jas. 5.11
This mercy enables us to have the hope of a reward: 2 Jn. v. 8


CONCL.:

The chorus reminds us of the need to keep on trusting in the Lord and looking to Him.

“Never give up, Never give up,
Never give up to thy sorrows,
Jesus will bid them depart;

Trust in the Lord, Trust in the Lord,
Sing when your trials are greatest,
Trust in the Lord and take heart.”

There are times when life become hard, and it would be easy to become so sad and desponding that one is tempted just to throw in the towel and quit. However, through His word, Jesus continues to speak to His people and tell them “Never Give Up.”

Note: This gospel song was one of Fanny’s later creations, published in 1903. Ira Allan Sankey was the son of Ira David Sankey, who was for many years associated with the ministry of Dwight L. Moody. The son became president of the Biglow and Main Publishing Company, responsible for publishing many of Fanny Crosby’s songs.

Some fifteen times (with the refrains) we have the word “never” here. Fanny is urging us not to abandon the work of the Lord, not to be a deserter of the cause. The emphasis reminded me of a famous speech given in October of 1941, by Winston Churchill. With the war raging in Europe, and England under attack, and having come through the Blitz (1940-41), he said to the boys at Harrow School:

“For everyone, surely, what we have gone through in this period–I am addressing myself to the School–surely from this period of ten months this is the lesson: never give in, never give in, never, never, never-in nothing, great or small, large or petty – never give in except to convictions of honour and good sense. Never yield to force; never yield to the apparently overwhelming might of the enemy.”

In the Bible, this kind of persistence is described with such expressions as persevering and being steadfast. Godly Job, in the Old Testament, is set before us a an example of perseverance in a time of severe trial and suffering (Jas. 5:11). And we are taught that, when we trust in the Lord as Job did, the eventual outcome of our trials can be a persevering (strong) faith (Rom. 5:3-4).

CH-1) Never be sad or desponding,
If thou hast faith to believe.
Grace, for the duties before thee,
Ask of thy God and receive.

Never give up, never give up,
Never give up to thy sorrows,
Jesus will bid them depart.
Trust in the Lord, trust in the Lord,
Sing when your trials are greatest,
Trust in the Lord and take heart.

One of the key areas needing this kind of steady persistence is in our prayer life. Sometimes there will be a temptation to give up, and stop praying, when the answer doesn’t come quickly, or it isn’t what we’d expect. But God’s Word counsels us to be “patient in tribulation, continuing steadfastly in prayer” (Rom. 12:12).

“Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, being watchful to this end with all perseverance and supplication for all the saints” (Eph. 6:18).

CH-2) What if thy burdens oppress thee;
What though thy life may be drear;
Look on the side that is brightest,
Pray, and thy path will be clear.

We would perhaps debate the unqualified optimism of this hymn at some points. CH-2 promises: “Pray, and thy path will be clear.” It isn’t always that cut and dried. As we pray, and persist in prayer, the way ahead may well become clearer. But because He wants us to continue trusting in Him, the future is sometimes left uncertain, and we simply need to cling to the Lord day by day.

Another area where perseverance is necessary is in resisting satanic temptation.

“Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil walks about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour. Resist him, steadfast in the faith, knowing that the same sufferings are experienced by your brotherhood in the world” (I Pet. 5:8-9).

Our service for Christ is another area that patience and “stick-to-it-iveness” is required, if we are to bear fruit for eternity.

“Therefore, my beloved brethren, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labour is not in vain in the Lord” (I Cor. 15:58).

CH-3) Never be sad or desponding,
There is a morrow for thee;
Soon thou shalt dwell in its brightness,
There with the Lord thou shalt be.

CH-4) Never be sad or desponding,
Lean on the arm of thy Lord;
Dwell in the depths of His mercy,
Thou shalt receive thy reward.
