亞倫的祝福  潘國華

 

經文：民數記第六章二十二至二十七節

　　為甚麼很多教會的主日崇拜程序都包括“祝福”這一項呢﹖這到底有沒有聖經根據呢﹖而它是否只是一種儀式而已﹖當牧師祝福後，會眾是否真的得着所祝的福呢﹖這一連串的問題都很有意思，不妨稍作討論。

　　從研讀民數記第六章二十二至二十七節，發現神吩咐亞倫和他的兒子去祝福以色列人。因此以色列人在會幕敬拜及其後來的聖殿敬拜中，都有“祝福”這項。自從以色列人被擄歸回，他們在會堂敬拜中，也保存“祝福”這項（Gordon J. Wenham, Numbers, TOTC, IVP,1981）。初期教會承受這傳統，所以在今日教會崇拜程序中，“祝福”一項也是合乎聖經及教會傳統的。

　　既然神吩咐亞倫和他的兒子祝福以色列人，故此，民數記第六章的祝福，被稱為“亞倫的祝福”或“祭司的祝福”。從第二十三節“你們要這樣為以色列人祝福，神表明祂必賜福以色列。因為他們是神的選民。今日，基督徒也因信成為神的兒女。誠如保羅所說：“你們既屬乎基督，就是亞伯拉罕的後裔，是照着應許承受產業的了。”（加三：29）因此，神同樣要賜福我們。究竟神要怎樣賜福以色列人呢﹖可從研究這祝詞的內容得知。

　　祝詞包括“三重祝福”，而每一重祝福又有兩個部分。茲以每一重祝福之重點分析之。

一．“亞倫的祝福”：包括保護──神的保護

　　祝詞的首句說：“願耶和華賜福給你，保護你。”（24節）神的賜福是無限量的，祂將一切福分和好處向我們傾倒。雖然這裏並沒有指明神賜甚麼福氣，但無疑包括一切福分。如：作神的兒女，產業，田地，健康及祂的同在。千萬不要以為我們之所以有各樣的物質享受，全靠自己的才幹或努力。信徒應以感恩的心情承認神的賜福。

　　既知道我們擁有的一切都是從神而來，就要感謝神的賜予；還得作神的管家，去管理神所賜的福分，還得去與人分享這些福分。因為：“多給誰，就向誰多取；多託誰，就要向誰多要。”“凡有的，還要加給他叫他有餘；沒有的，連他所有的，也要奪過來。”（路一二：48，太二五：29）另一祝詞的部份是神的“保謢”。詩人說：“耶和華要保護你，免受一切的災害。祂要保護你的性命，你出，你入，耶和華要保護你，從今時直到永遠。”（詩一二一：7-8）既知神會保護，我們就不用再憂心戚戚了。應將一切憂慮交給神，祂必看顧，保護。

　　有否為神保護我們的性命而感謝祂呢﹖神保護我們好像保護眼中的瞳人：若不是祂准許，甚至一根頭髮也不會掉落：無論大小事情或動作，神都看顧保護我們。正如保羅指出：“我們生活，動作，存留，都在乎神…”（徒一七：28）

二．“亞倫的祝福”：包括赦免──神的赦免

　　這祝福的第二句乃：“願耶和華使祂的臉光照你，賜恩給你。”（25節）同樣，這包括：

　　1. “神的臉光照你”：這句話似暗示神好像春天的太陽，光照大地。當然這只是隱喻。當春日陽光普照大地之時，是非常愉快和溫暖的。神以祂的臉光照我們，亦同樣表明神接納我們：祂的慈愛使我們感到歡愉和溫馨。

　　既然神以臉光照我們，以笑臉迎着我們，故此，神一定會賜恩我們，祂必將我們從一切患難，困苦中拯救出來。神又必聽我們向祂的哀求，將我們從仇敵手中，病患中，甚至罪惡中救拔出來。因為祂要賜恩給我們。

　　神所給的恩典，莫如赦罪的大恩。正如以賽亞所說：“你們的罪雖像硃紅，必變成雪白，雖紅如丹顏，必白如羊毛。”（賽一：18）“天離地何等的高，祂的慈愛向敬畏祂的人，也是何等的大。東離西有多遠，祂叫我們的過犯，離我們也有多遠。”（詩一○三：11-12）可見，神接納又赦免我們的罪。可惜，今天不少基督徒往往不願意饒恕他人的過失。求神光照我們，看到祂的慈愛，恩典。叫我們也好像神一樣廣行赦免，饒恕得罪我們的人。

　　不錯，神必賜恩又會赦免我們所承認的罪。很可惜，我們卻不知自己的罪；我們對罪的敏感性漸漸失去，對道德標準也慢慢降低。從前不可思議的罪惡，今日竟然變成司空見慣的事。我們的良知亦被世俗風氣所蒙蔽。當心！但願我們都如大衛一樣能多自省：“神啊，求你鑒察我，知道我的心思，試煉我，知道我的意念。看在我裏面有甚麼惡行沒有，引導我走永生的道路。”（詩一三九：23-24）

三．“亞倫的祝福”：包括平安──神賜的平安

　　祝福的第三句指出：“願耶和華向你仰臉，賜你平安。”（26節）同樣，這句話包括兩部分：（1）神向你仰臉；（2）神賜你平安。

　　究竟“神使祂的臉光照你”和“神向你仰臉”有何分別﹖前者強調神的接納和慈愛，後者偏重神注目於我們。當有人向你仰起臉來，意即他正注視着你。照樣，神向我們仰臉，即祂注視着我們，祂的眼目時常看顧我們。為甚麼﹖這種行動表示祂愛和關懷我們。正如一對戀人，他們往往注視對方。又好像一位母親，她的眼目時常放在她心愛的兒女身上。

　　當神注目我們時，祂必看顧我們的需要，供給我們，補足我們的缺乏。“耶和華的眼目，看顧敬畏祂的人，和仰望祂慈愛的人。”（詩三三：18）

　　既然神注視我們，向我們仰臉；祂必樂意將平安賜給我們。“平安”，在聖經的用字來說，不但指國泰民安，更指財富，豐足，健康及救恩。故此“平安”包括神給予祂的兒女最好的禮物之總和。由此可知，神對我們的恩典多麼豐厚無量！

　　在這三重的祝福裏，祝詞是朝向一個高潮的。而這個高潮就是神將最好的禮物──平安賜給我們。在主耶穌將離世之際，安慰門徒說：“我留下平安給你，我將我的平安賜給你們。我所賜的，不像世人所賜的，你們心裏不要憂愁，也不要膽怯。”（約一四：27）今天，神依然將平安賜給我們。在這個動蕩不安的世代裏，人類最大的需要是平安。

結語

　　亞倫的祝福包括保護，赦免和平安。究竟是否真的可以得着這些祝福﹖神保證說：“他們要如此奉我的名，為以色列人祝福，我也要賜福他們。”（民六：27）難道神只祝福以色列人，不也更祝福祂的兒女──“真以色列人”麼﹖所以，凡到神面前的人，必須憑信心去接受祂的福分（來一一：6）。

　　為甚麼神要祝福我們呢﹖有一次一位小女孩走到她母親身旁問道：“母親，你為甚麼愛我﹖”母親放下她的工作，將女兒抱在懷裏，輕輕地對她說：“不為甚麼，只因你是我的女兒！”為甚麼神要如此祝福我們呢﹖同樣，神告訴我們說：“不為甚麼，只因你們是我的兒女！”

　　“神為愛祂的人所預備的，是眼睛未曾看見，耳朵未曾聽見，人心也未曾想到的。”（林前二：9）

"The Lord bless you and keep you" is a classical sacred choral composition by John Rutter, based on Numbers 6:24-26. It is a setting of a biblical benediction, followed by an extended "Amen". Rutter scored the piece for four vocal parts (SATB) and organ.[1] He composed it in 1981 for the memorial service of Edward T. Chapman, the director of music at Highgate School, London, with whom he had studied when he attended the school.[2]

It was published by Oxford University Press in 1981, in the anthology Oxford Easy Anthems, edited by David Willcocks.

Text[edit]

"The Lord bless you and keep you" is a setting of the Priestly Blessing, also known as the Aaronic blessing, from the Book of Numbers in the Bible (Numbers 6:24–26). The blessing, sung or spoken, is used at the conclusion of worship, baptism, ordination, marriage, and other special occasions in Christian worship.[3]

Other settings[edit]

In 1900, Peter Lutkin composed a setting for a cappella choir.[3]

Music[edit]

Rutter's setting is for four vocal parts (SATB) and organ.[1] Marked "Andante espressivo", the music is in G-flat major and common time. It takes about 2+1⁄2 minutes to perform. Rutter also wrote an arrangement for soprano, alto and keyboard in F major[4] and a version for choir and orchestra.[2]

In "The Lord bless you and keep you", Rutter keeps the music restrained and simple. The accompaniment first rests on a pedal point; long chords in the bass change only every half bar, while broken chords in steady quavers add colour. The first line of the text is sung by the sopranos alone, then repeated by all voices, starting in unison but expanding to harmony on the words "The Lord make His face to shine upon you". "The Lord lift His countenance upon you" is sung twice in two-part homophony, first soprano and alto, then tenor and bass. "And give you peace" appears three times, softer each time from mp to pp, first in the soprano, then in the tenor, and finally in unison in all voices.[5] A polyphonic "Amen" grows to the climax of the music both in range and in intensity, then gradually softens and, after a "molto rallentando", reaches the final long chord.[2][5]

Performances[edit]

Rutter has chosen this work to represent his compositions in workshops.[6] It was part of the celebration of the 100th birthday of the Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother in 2000,[2] and was sung by the choir of St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle at the wedding of the Duke and Duchess of Sussex in 2018.

Recordings[edit]

The piece has been recorded several times,[2] for example ending a collection of Rutter's choral works performed under his direction by the Cambridge Singers and the City of London Sinfonia.[7] It also concluded the 2010 recording of the composer's Requiem by Polyphony and the Bournemouth Sinfonietta, conducted by Stephen Layton.[8] In his notes to that recording, the composer described his musical aims: "I happen not to believe in erecting needless barriers between composer and listener: given a choice between critical approbation and a chance of touching the hearts of people outside the limited circle of contemporary music aficionados, I know which I prefer."[9]

The Lord bless you and keep you  (by Peter C. Lutkin, 1858-1931)

Notes

Scripture References:
st. = Num. 6:24-26

The words of “The LORD Bless You and Keep You’ come entirely from Numbers 6:24-26 (RSV), well known as the priestly blessing and the Aaronic benediction. Martin Bucer and John Calvin introduced the Aaronic blessing to Reformed worship after the example set by Martin Luther's Formula Missae ("Formula of the Mass," 1523).

Liturgical Use:
Conclusion of worship, in which case it need not be repeated by the pastor; also appropriate during baptism, ordination, marriage, and other special occasions in Christian worship.

--Psalter Hymnal Handbook, 1988

 

Notes  Peter Christian Lutkin

BENEDICTION was composed in 1900 by Peter Christian Lutkin (b. Thompsonville, WI, 1858; d. Evanston, IL, 1931). This music is more an anthem than a hymn; it was called a "Farewell Anthem with Sevenfold Amen." Without the original series of "Amens," the current setting is accessible to congregations. Choirs may still love to sing the entire anthem–or if the choir wishes to sing just the blessing, the congregation can respond with a simple "Amen" cadence. Sing this music with a sense that its text is divinely ordained in the manner of an authoritative proclamation. The song's inner parts require good leadership from the choir for well-executed congregational singing. The popularity of this song can be attributed in part to its use for many years at the end of the weekly radio broadcasts of the Back to God Hour, an international ministry of the Christian Reformed Church.

Orphaned at an early age, Lutkin was raised in Chicago and had his early musical training in the choir school of the St. James Episcopal Cathedral. He studied under prominent organ teachers in Chicago, continued his education in Europe (1881-1884), and earned a doctorate in music from Syracuse University in 1900. In Chicago he served as organist and choirmaster at St. Clements Episcopal Church (1884-1891) and St. James Cathedral (1891-1896) and taught music theory at the American Conservatory (1885-1895). Lutkin was one of the founders of the American Guild of Organists. He also established the Chicago North Shore Festivals and founded the Northwestern University School of Music, of which he was the first dean (1896-1920). At several different times Lutkin was president of the Music Teacher's National Association. A composer of organ and choral music, he served on the editorial committees for both the Methodist Hymnal (1905) and the Episcopal Hymnal (1918).

--Psalter Hymnal Handbook
