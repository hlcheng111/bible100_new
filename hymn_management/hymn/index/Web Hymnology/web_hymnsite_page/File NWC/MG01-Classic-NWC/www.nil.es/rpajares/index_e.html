Welcome! You will find in this page classical music files in two different formats:

  • MIDI
  • NWC (by NoteWorthy Composer)

MIDI files can be played by clicking on the link, but you can also download them to your hard disk (right-click on them and select Save Link or Save Target).

NWC files are internally compressed. You'll need release 1.70 of either NoteWorthy Player (freeware) or Composer (shareware) to play these files.

All these sequences have been transcribed by me. They are free and 'open source'. If you use them publicly, a bit of credit will be appreciated.

MIDI is the preferred format since it allows for several optimizations not possible in NWC format.

All sequences are optimized for a Sound Blaster AWE 64 or Live! with the 8 MB E-MU General MIDI soundfont. (Live! users: please select Default Reverb at 100%, if you can.)

Composers are sorted by date of birth. Century assignments depend on composers' flourishing time, estimated 30 years later after their birth.

This web site is willingly limited to a single page.

If you have had the patience to listen to any of my sequences and you wish to share with me your comments, even negative and critical ones, please do write to me. I will appreciate it. Send your comments to rpajares@rpajares.net




Collections

  • Classical MIDI Archives, the biggest reservoir of classical music files in MIDI format. What you don't find here possibly hasn't been sequenced. If you're in Europe you'll achieve better access from mirrors in Austria, Sweden or the United Kingdom.
  • Classical MIDI Connection, another good collection of classical music files.
  • Classical musical MIDI, a collection of classical and present day --but classically inspired-- music files informally managed by Reg.
  • Classical MIDI Resource is just the opposite of the previous site. This collection presents itself as select and exclusive.
  • String ensemble café, a site managed by Masashi Idogawa and devoted mainly to classical string sequences, which houses some nice works not seen elsewhere.
  • NWC Scriptorium, a collection of music files in NWC format. Not all of them are classical music.

Sequencers

  • JUN-MIDI Philarmonic Orchestra. Presently Jun Nishio, from Japan, is in my opinion simply the best classical music sequencer with a home page. Don't miss his "Scherezade".
  • Espace-MIDI houses the outstanding and polished sequences of Jean-François Lucarelli. They are more and more and their quality is better and better.
  • Robert Finley's classical MIDI page. His great piano solo performances are better than his orchestral concerti.
  • The Virtual Philharmonic Orchestra, houses the excellent sequences of Reinhold Behringer in a web site built around a bright and happy metaphor.
  • Classical MIDI Files by David Siu. David is one of the most prolific sequencers and his MIDI file collection is very interesting.
  • Spanish Early Music MIDI Files, a superb collection of pieces by Renaissance and Early Baroque Spanish composers, sequenced and documented by Francisco Villanueva.
  • The George Pollen MIDI Collection, big and prolific. Interesting sequences of British composers, in particular of Arthur Sullivan. Don't miss Ronald Binge's works.
  • Fred Nachbaur's Dogstar Music. Houses his own compositions and his creative sequences of classical and not so classical works. Very interesting musical experiments by means of Noteworthy Composer.

Music and MIDI

  • Zarzuela! It's amazing, but the best web site on zarzuela is in English! Don't forget to visit it if you want to know what sort of musical genre the Spanish zarzuela is.
  • Table of Transposing Instruments for a symphonic orchestra.
  • Physics of sound. Not only physics but the best explanation of musical scales and other concepts related to musical perception.
  • MIDI Technical Fanatic's Brainwashing Center. Informal and humorous presentation of some of MIDI's deep technicalities.
  • PrintedMusic.com or the efficient way of buying unobtainable scores.
  • J. Meyer's Orchestral Composition: Guidelines, Techniques and Devices, very important knowledge base to undertake full orchestra transcriptions.
  • Live! Center, a very well done site which can't be ignored by any owner of a Sound Blaster Live! soundcard.
  • Filomúsica. An interesting on-line magazine about music and philosophy (in Spanish only).
