＠. Rhythm. 

The rhythm of any hymn tune is not necessarily dependent upon the meter per se (meter in this context refers to the poetic meter -- how many syllables of text per poetic line). Tunes with the same poetic meter can have any number of variations in melodic rhythm. Consider the following tunes, all in Common Meter (8.6.8.6):

l   AMAZING GRACE   (NEW BRITAIN ) (MH 378)

l   ST. ANNE (MH 117)

l   WINCHESTER OLD  (MH 603) -- Is this a good text/tune match?

l   LAND OF REST (American)   (MH 269)

No particular combinations of rhythmic values can be said to be more or less effective in the success of a given hymn tune. However, certain rhythmic combinations can have a definite effect on the ability of a tune to be remembered and consequently contribute to its continued use. Consider the rhythm of the following tunes:

l   SHOWALTER  [What a fellowship, what a joy divine]  (MH 133) -- Leaning on the everlasting arms s

＠　Main intervals

 

The table shows the most widely used conventional names for the intervals between the notes of a chromatic scale. A perfect unison (also known as perfect prime)[5] is an interval formed by two identical notes. Its size is zero cents. A semitone is any interval between two adjacent notes in a chromatic scale, a whole tone is an interval spanning two semitones (for example, a major second), and a tritone is an interval spanning three tones, or six semitones (for example, an augmented fourth).[a] Rarely, the term ditone is also used to indicate an interval spanning two whole tones (for example, a major third), or more strictly as a synonym of major third.

Intervals with different names may span the same number of semitones, and may even have the same width. For instance, the interval from D to F♯ is a major third, while that from D to G♭ is a diminished fourth. However, they both span 4 semitones. If the instrument is tuned so that the 12 notes of the chromatic scale are equally spaced (as in equal temperament), these intervals also have the same width. Namely, all semitones have a width of 100 cents, and all intervals spanning 4 semitones are 400 cents wide.

The names listed here cannot be determined by counting semitones alone. The rules to determine them are explained below. Other names, determined with different naming conventions, are listed in a separate section. Intervals smaller than one semitone (commas or microtones) and larger than one octave (compound intervals) are introduced below.

I. MELODY. 

The most important musical aspect of a hymn is the MELODY because it is through the TUNE that the words are conveyed, internalized and proclaimed. Any melody consists of individual pitches organized according to some traditional SCALE or MODE. Here are examples of tunes which demonstrate a variety of scales and modes:

 

 

圣诗的旋律（或称曲调）是圣诗音乐最主要的部分。

旋律是由音的高低和音的节奏所构成。现代圣诗多数用大调式(major)或小调式(minor)。

O Come, O Come, Emmanuel) 　VENI EMMANUEL (Chant)「以马内利来临歌」（普93，颂新94，颂87，生106）。

Steps and skips

 

Main article: Steps and skips

Linear (melodic) intervals may be described as steps or skips. A step, or conjunct motion,[14] is a linear interval between two consecutive notes of a scale. Any larger interval is called a skip (also called a leap), or disjunct motion.[14] In the diatonic scale,[d] a step is either a minor second (sometimes also called half step) or major second (sometimes also called whole step), with all intervals of a minor third or larger being skips.

For example, C to D (major second) is a step, whereas C to E (major third) is a skip.

@ 2．赞美诗与调式

 

赞美诗与调式也有密切关系。赞美诗的调一般是不轻易更改的，因每首赞美诗所使用的调都是经过仔细和认真考虑的。为了便于会众歌唱，用于会众颂唱的赞美诗一般少转调，但有时出于唱诗班献唱的需要，同时也为了适应不同人声、不同声部和不同乐器的需要，会将一首赞美诗从原调转到另一个调，以丰富圣乐的表现力，

因为“移调”能改变色彩并丰富音色，当从前面一个调转向后面一个调时会给人耳目一新的体验和感受。

Form 

in music has to do with how a piece is organized into sections. For example,

the tune HYMN TO JOY (MH 89), can be divided into four distinct sections: A A' B A'. What is the sectional organization of the following tunes:

l   MH 103 ST. DENIO

l   MH 102 NUN DANKET

l   MH 697 AMERICA

For the most part, the individual "sections" of a tune which correspond to the letters as described above (i.e., AABA), are synonymous with the individual poetic verses (lines). These can also be called musical PHRASES.

A phrase in music is "the shortest passage demonstrating a complete musical thought and having a point relative repose (i.e., cadence)." [From Green, Form in Tonal Music]

Word painting

Word painting flourished well into the Baroque music period. One famous, well-known example occurs in Handel's Messiah, where a tenor aria contains Handel's setting of the text:

Every valley shall be exalted, and every mountain and hill made low; the crooked straight, and the rough places plain. (Isaiah 40:4)

In Handel's melody, the word "valley" ends on a low note, "exalted" is a rising figure; "mountain" forms a peak in the melody, and "hill" a smaller one, while "low" is another low note. "Crooked" is sung to a rapid figure of four different notes, while "straight" is sung on a single note, and in "the rough places plain," "the rough places" is sung over short, separate notes whereas the final word "plain" is extended over several measures in a series of long notes. 四行（四句），每行有八个音节（长乐律）

圣诗学者形容歌词与曲的配搭好像婚姻一样，它们必须在乐律、重音、情绪和搭配方面协调。如果一首圣诗有四行（四句），每行有八个音节（长乐律），那么，它的旋律也应该有四个乐句，并且有适合唱八个音节的旋律音。调式也应该和歌词的思想内容相配。[2] 

 

音节

汉字是单音节的，英文字则是多音节。

 

轻重音节(accent) 與汉字有声的区别

汉字有声的区别，如：普通话有四个声，广州话有九个声。英语却没有声的区别，只有轻重音节(accent)之分。汉语如果用错了声，一个字可能变成另外的字，例如普通话的「四」，是第四声（即去声），「死」字是第三声（即上声），虽然拼音都是si，但用错了声，「四人」说歪了，变成「死人」。另外中文还有一个同音异字的问题，如：「人」和「仁」；「话」和「画」等。在配曲，填词和翻译时都要注意，以免造成错觉。 

 

唱歌呼吸

词与曲搭配的另一个问题，是乐句和语句的一致性。唱歌是要呼吸的。那怕是极短的吸气，吸气时声音都要中断一下。但一个词、词组或某些语句的当中是不应该中断的。有些诗班在唱「主在圣殿中」时，把「都应当肃静」唱成「都应，当肃静」，这是不对的。「都应」不是一个词，不能在「应」字的后面停下来吸气。但是旋律的本身也有一点问题，因为「应」这个字有两拍，后面的「肃」字又有四拍，「静」字也有两拍。没有经过声乐训练的人不能一口气把句子唱完，于是在「应」字后面吸气了。 

 

6 | 5 3 1 2 | 3 – 3 4 | 2 - – -|1 - 0 

在 主 面 前 都 应 当 肃 静。 

 

吸气

圣诗的词或曲的作者都应该注意这种问题。圣诗的译者也同样要注意吸气的问题。有一首圣诗：「在主怀中歌」(曲名：CRIMOND，普29，颂359，颂新85，生312)，歌词是诗篇二十三篇——最为人熟悉和喜爱的一首大卫诗，旋律也很流畅，本来可以成为一首非常好的圣诗，但是英文歌词由于搭配不妥，造成换气上的困难。全歌共有五段歌词，每段唱下去都有问题。因此，近几年大部分新版的英文圣诗集已经割爱了。不过，在译成中文后，却不成问题，呼吸问题很容易解决，所以中文圣诗集都保留这首诗。 

 

词等强拍

在填词或翻译时还应注意重要的词，如：名词、动词、形容词和副词等应放在强拍。如果是双字词或多字词，如「耶稣」、「耶和华」、「耶路撒冷」等词，应把第一个字放在强拍上。助词、连词如「的」、「了」、「和」、「呢」等，则应放在弱拍。这是为了让听众容易听清楚所唱的内容。

 

节奏感和音乐性，押韵

当圣诗没有曲谱，拿来朗诵的时候，为了使诗具有节奏感和音乐性，押韵是必要的。不过，今天圣诗都谱上曲调，是用来歌唱，不是用来朗诵的，对听者来说，旋律比歌词的韵律更为突出。这样，圣诗的押韵便不再是一个很重要的问题。所以，现代的圣诗很多都是自由诗体，只要能体现出歌词的意思，在配音上又注意到上面提过的问题，作词和翻译押不押韵都无关重要。当然，如果既能押韵又能表达原意，那是最理想的结果。但是如果为了歌词的押韵而削弱了诗词的含义，则宁可放弃押韵而追求意境的完美表达。

 

概括而言，圣诗与圣经有着密切的关系。圣诗的内容或直接引用圣经，或以圣经为基础。圣诗渗透着神学思想。不同的神学观点在圣诗里都能反映出来。圣诗的体裁从描写的对象去分析，有「主观」和「客观」两种。主观体裁的圣诗描写或抒发个人的感情。客观体裁的圣诗描写的对象是神，圣诗的内容，体裁和风格与它们所处的时代相关。如现代的人权思想和妇女解放运动，对圣诗的写作和评价都产生一定的影响。圣诗根据它们的思想内容分别有各种形式，如：歌颂、祈祷、对话、灵修和训勉等。圣诗在文学的结构上有格律、韵律和乐律等特征。圣诗在曲调格式上属「分节歌曲」，即各节歌词唱同一个旋律。每首圣诗的旋律都有一定的调式和节奏，圣诗除了根据内容而定的标题，还有曲名。曲名可以是人名、地名、时节，或借用一首曲名，词牌名等。由于音乐和语言的各自特点，在写作歌词和配曲时，应注意词与曲的搭配合适，以免产生听觉上的混淆。还要考虑歌的可唱性，把旋律的音域限制在一般人容易发音的范围内。

Folk tunes

 

Carol.

A type of hymn and hymn tune whose words and music are typically joyful and festive and many times associated with the celebration of Christmas. A carol may also be associated with other devotional songs of a joyful character, such as Easter. In the 13th, 14th and 15th centuries, the humanizing influence of St. Francis of Assisi helped produce a great flowering of this type of religious folk song which in a real sense is the late medieval equivalent of the modern pop-gospel song, contemporary Christian song or praise and worship chorus.
