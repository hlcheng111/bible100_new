　O Gott Vater, wir loben dich (Ausbund Hymn No.131)

1 O God and Father, Thee we bless,
Thy goodness ever praise addressing,
That Thou, O Lord, with graciousness,
Anew us kindly manifesting,
Hast led us thus together, Lord,
To us admonish through Thy living Word;
For this grant grace and constant blessing!

2 Open Thy servants' lips, O Lord,
And grant them wisdom, and Thy power,
That they rightly may speak Thy Word,
That holy lives may spring and flower
And glorify Thee for Thy good,
O grant us hunger for such sacred food;
This is our fervent pray'r this hour!

3 An understanding mind impart,
Inspire us, Lord, we here do pray Thee,
That Thy Word known within our hearts,
In holy lives may ever praise Thee;
And in true righteousness, that we,
Thy gracious Word so heeding constantly,
May undeceived obey and serve Thee!

4 Thine only is the kingdom, Lord,
Thine only is the power given;
Within Thy church with one accord
Thy name we praise, and raise to heaven!
And from our heart's depth, Lord, we pray,
Be with us in this hallowed hour today,
Through Jesus Christ, our Savior, Amen! Amen.

Greitter, Matthäus, was a monk and chorister of Strassburg Cathedral, but in 1524 espoused the cause of the Reformation. In 1528 he was appointed assistant pastor of St. Martin's Church, and afterwards at St. Stephen's. When the Interim [Agricola] was forced on Strassburg, he was the only one of the Lutheran pastors that sought to further it, a course which he afterwards deeply regretted. His death is dated by Wetzel, i. 349, as Dec. 20, 1550; by the Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie, ix. 636, as Nov. 20, 1550; while Koch, ii. 104, says he died of the pestilence in 1552.

Greitter was a distinguished musician, and with his friend Dachstein (q.v.) edited the Strassburg Kirchen ampt, 1524-5. Four psalm tunes by Greitter, and one by Dachstein were inserted by Calvin in his first Hymnbook published at Strassburg, 1539. All these were transferred to the first edition of the French-Genevan Psalter in 1542, and two of them, both by Greitter (the tunes to psalms 36 and 91), were retained in the final edition of 1562.
