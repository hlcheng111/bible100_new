Born at Leeds, Yorkshire, England, he attended Gainford Academy, Rotherham College, Nottingham Institute, and Cheshunt College. He became pastor at Great Marlow, Buckinghamshire. Leaving the clerical field due to ill health, he conducted evangelistic campaigns in Britain (1874-82) and worldwide (1882-1889). An author, he wrote a number of books. I Am His and He Is Mine

Note: This hymn is also called Loved with Everlasting Love. James Mountain wrote the tune specifically for these words, when the song was first published in 1876.

It doesn’t have to be divine love to teach us some great lessons. Guy meets gal and they fall in love. Suddenly, “Heaven above is softer blue, earth around is sweeter green” (CH-2). Everything sparkles with new life and beauty, as though they’re seeing it clearly for the first time. And if that is true of what happens when we experience the love of another human being, to what infinite degree can it be lifted in the experience of God’s love.

Sadly, human love may not endure, but God’s always will. The Bible gives us a long list of things that might, in the thinking of some, cause us to lose the blessing of His love, saying, “[No, none of these] shall be able to separate us from the love of god which is in Christ Jesus our Lord” (Rom. 8:35-39).

Missionary to India, Henry Martyn (1781-1812) wrote in his diary of the transforming power God’s love in saving him. In fact, George Robinson may have got the idea for his hymn from there. Martyn wrote:

“Since I have known God in a saving manner, painting, poetry, and music have had charms unknown to me before; for religion has refined my mind, and made it susceptible of impression from the sublime and beautiful.”

The perception of God’s love comes to us through His Spirit-inspired Word, and through the gracious ministrations of the Spirit in the believer’s heart.

CH-1) Loved with everlasting love, led by grace that love to know;
Gracious Spirit from above, Thou hast taught me it is so!
O this full and perfect peace! O this transport all divine!
In a love which cannot cease, I am His, and He is mine.
In a love which cannot cease, I am His, and He is mine.

And it’s not only the world of nature that is seen with new eyes when our souls begin to appreciate the depth and enduring nature of the love of God. In addition, things that once worried and bothered us are seen in a new way. Like John, the disciple “whom Jesus loved” resting on His bosom (Jn. 13:23), we find comfort in intimate fellowship with Him.

CH-3) Things that once were wild alarms cannot now disturb my rest;
Closed in everlasting arms, pillowed on the loving breast.
O to lie forever here, doubt and care and self resign,
While He whispers in my ear, I am His, and He is mine.
While He whispers in my ear, I am His, and He is mine.

Human frailty being what it is, we aren’t always worry free–an idealistic idea which might be taken from Robinson’s third stanza. But we do learn to cast our cares upon the Lord, with the knowledge that He will lovingly care for His own (I Pet. 5:7).

We first encounter divine love at Calvary. He loved us so much that He sent His Son to die in our place, bearing the penalty of our sin. “In this is love, not that we loved God, but that He loved us and sent His Son to be the propitiation for our sins” (I Jn. 4:10; cf. Jn. 3:16). We cling to the cross, and claim the remedy we need, by faith.

Then, as we go on in our Christian lives, we learn more. Paul’s prayer for the Ephesian believers is a suitable prayer for each of us:

“[That we] may be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the width and length and depth and height–to know the love of Christ which passes knowledge; that [we] may be filled with all the fullness of God” (Eph. 3:18-19).

“[God] has loved us and given us everlasting consolation and good hope by grace” (II Thess. 2:16). Who could ask for more that to be loved forever by Almighty God?

CH-4) His forever, only His; who the Lord and me shall part?
Ah, with what a rest of bliss Christ can fill the loving heart!
Heav’n and earth may fade and flee, firstborn light in gloom decline;
But while God and I shall be, I am His, and He is mine.
But while God and I shall be, I am His, and He is mine.

Questions:
1) Why does God love us so much?

2) What are some particular manifestations of the love of God in your life, right now?

I AM HIS AND HE IS MINE

　
There are numerous hymns that I really miss singing and hearing. This week's is one of those. I have always loved this hymn because of the rich words and the beautiful harmony. Years ago my wife and I sang this as part of a mixed quartete and it was a blessing to sing it. It is also known to some as "Loved with Everlasting Love." The words were written by George W. Robinson (1838 - 1877) but little is known about him. Born in Ireland, Robinson was educated at Dublin's Trinity College, and later at New College in London. Later he became a pastor in Dudley. It is said that he became ill and resigned as pastor. Little is known about the cause of his illness and death. but this great hymn was written one year before his death which came at the young age of 39. The words do communicate that Robinson knew the Lord and knew where he was going after his death. He talks about this full and perfect peace which he obviously received from the Lord. I especially love the poetic words of the second verse which share what a believer can see that Christless eyes can never experience. And what a beautiful picture shared in verse three where the child of God is sheltered in the everlasting arms of the Father while the wild alarms of life are all around. The words of the fourth verse are even more powerful when one realizes that the author was ill and just a year away from death but realizing that nothing could part him from the Lord. What precious, powerful words. And what a joy to know that "I am His and He is mine!"

“I AM HIS, AND HE IS MINE”

“My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me” (Jn. 10:27)

     INTRO.: 

A hymn which exults in the fact that as Christ’s sheep we hear His voice and follow Him so that He knows us is “I Am His, and He Is Mine.”  The text was written by George Wade Robinson, who was born in 1838 at Cork, Ireland, and was educated at Trinity College, Dublin, and at New College, St. John’s Wood, London.  Becoming a Congregational minister, he began his work with Dr. Urwick at the York St. Chapel in Dublin.  Later, he served at St. John’s Wood in Dudley, and at Union St. in Brighton, both in England.  Also, he published two volumes of verse, Songs in God’s World and Loveland.  This hymn first appeared in the 1876 Hymns of Consecration and Faith, although it is possible that Robinson had completed it in 1870.  The tune (Everlasting Love) was composed James Mountain (1844-1933). The editor of the first edition of Hymns of Consecration and Faith, Mountain provided this melody to fit these words by Robinson for inclusion in that compilation, although some books give the date of 1890, which may be a later edition of Hymns of Consecration and Faith.  Robinson died on Jan. 18, 1877, at Southampton, England.

Among hymnbooks published by members of the Lord’s church during the twentieth century for use in churches of Christ, the song may be found in the 1990 Songs of the Church 21st C. Ed. (which uses an arrangement copyright in 1985 by Laurel Press), and the 1994 Songs of Faith and Praise both edited by Alton H. Howard; and the 1992 Songs of Faith and Praise edited by John P. Wiegand.  Among other hymnbooks in my collection, I have seen the song in the 1951 Inspiring Hymns, the 1968 Great Hymns of the Faith, and the 1979 Praise: Our Songs and Hymns, all published by Singspiration Music; the 1967 Favorite Hymns of Praise published by Tabernacle Publishing Company; the 1972 Living Hymns published by Encore Publications Inc.; the 1972 Soul Stirring Songs and Hymns published by Sword of the Lord Publishers; the 1974 Hymns of the Living Church published by Hope Publishing Company; the 1976 New Church Hymnal published by Lexicon Music Inc.; and the 2006 Christian Life Hymnal published by Hendrickson Worship.

The song identifies some of the blessings that come with belonging to Christ.

I. Stanza 1 mentions His love

“Loved with everlasting love, Led by grace that love to know;

Gracious Spirit from above, Thou hast taught me it is so!

O this full and perfect peace!  O this transport all divine!

In a love which cannot cease, I am His, and He is mine.”

  1. God has loved us with an everlasting love: Jer. 31:3
  2. The Spirit, who was sent from above to guide the apostles into all truth, has taught us that this is so through the word which He inspired them to write: Jn. 16:7-13
  3. This love cannot cease because the very nature of the eternal God is love: 1 Jn. 4:7-10

II. Stanza 2 mentions His joy

“Heaven above is softer blue, Earth around is sweeter green!

Something lives in every hue Christless eyes have never seen;

Birds with gladder songs o’er-flow, Flowers with deeper beauties shine,

Since I know, as now I know, I am His, and He is mine.”

  1. God has placed things both in the heavens above and on the earth below to point us to Him: Ps. 19:1, 24:1
  2. While all mankind can benefit from these blessings, only those who belong to Christ can truly and fully appreciate these things: 1 Cor. 3:22-23
  3. Therefore, as birds with glad songs overflow, so the Christian can always rejoice in the Lord: Phil. 4:4

III. Stanza 3 mentions His peace

“Things that once were wild alarms Cannot now disturb my rest,

Closed in everlasting arms, Pillowed on the loving breast.

O to lie forever here, Doubt and care and self resign,

While He whispers in my ear, I am His, and He is mine.”

  1. The trials and tribulations of life are often wild alarms that disturb our rest: Acts 14:22
  2. However, such things no longer disturb the Christian because he is closed in everlasting arms: Deut. 33:27
  3. Hence, we can resign doubt and care because we have the peace of God that passes all understanding: Phil. 4:6-7

IV. Stanza 4 mentions His hope

“His forever, only His; Who the Lord and me shall part?

Ah, with what a rest of bliss Christ can fill the loving heart!

Heaven and earth may fade and flee, Firstborn light in gloom decline;

But while God and I shall be, I am His, and He is mine.”

  1. Our hope is the result of the fact that if we cling to Christ, nothing can separate us from His love: Rom. 8:35-39
  2. Therefore, we look forward to resting from our labors when this life is over: Rev. 14:13
  3. This hope extends even beyond that time when heaven and earth shall pass away, because we look for a new heaven and new earth: 2 Pet. 3:10-13

CONCL.: 

The last two lines of each stanza are repeated as a sort of refrain.  This world seems to take great delight in thinking of Christians as weak people who turn to God as a kind of crutch and use their religion only as a means of escape because they cannot really cope with life.  Nothing could be further from the truth.  The fact of the matter is that the people of this world simply cannot even begin to comprehend the genuine love, peace, joy, and hope that I have in Christ because “I Am His, and He Is Mine.”
