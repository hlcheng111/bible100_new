    He’ll help you along, help you along;

    Just follow gladly where He leadeth,
    His gentle voice obey.

    The burden that has seemed so heavy,
    Take to the Lord in prayer.
  4. Just lean upon the arms of Jesus,
    Then leave all to Him, leave all to Him;
    His heart is full of love and mercy,
    His eyes are never dim.

Short Name: Lewis E. Jones
Full Name: Jones, Lewis E. (Lewis Edgar) 1865-1936
Birth Year: 1865
Death Year: 1936

Lewis Edgar Jones USA 1865-1936.

 Born in Yates City, IL, his family moved near Davenport, IA, where he lived on a farm until age 21. He went into business for awhile, and attended the Moody Bible Institute in Chicago. He was a classmate of evangelist, Billy Sunday. After graduating, he worked for the YMCA in Davenport, IA; Fort Worth, TX (1915, as general secretary); and Santa Barbara, CA (1925 as general secretary). Hymn writing was his avocation, and he wrote 200+, advising that many came from pastors’ sermons. He married Lora May Wright (1872-1950), and they had a daughter, Frances Ellen (1897-1982). He died in Santa Barbara, CA.

John Perry

　

Lean on His Arm  (Franklin Lycurgus Eiland)

“LEAN ON HIS ARM”

“Trust in the Lord with all thine heart…” (Prov. 3:5)

     INTRO.: 

A song which encourages us to trust in the Lord with all our hearts is “Lean on His Arm.”  The text was written by Franklin Lycurgus Eiland, who was born in Noxumbee County, MS, on Mar. 25, 1860, and died at Golden, TX, on Dec. 3, 1909, at the age of 49. 

He is known for such songs as “Don’t You Want to Be Ready to Go?” and the tune for “Hold to God’s Unchanging Hand.”  The tune was composed by Will M. Ramsey (1872-1939).  The song is dated 1904.  Ramsey also provided both words and music for another song that has appeared in some of our books, “He Whispers Sweet Peace to Me.”  Among hymnbooks published by members of the Lord’s church during the twentieth century for use in churches of Christ, “Lean on His Arm” appeared in the 1978 Hymns of Praise edited by Reuel Lemmons.  Today it may be found in the 1971 Songs of the Church, the 1990 Songs of the Church 21st C. Ed., and the 1994 Songs of Faith and Praise all edited by Alton H. Howard; the 1983 edition of the 1978 Church Gospel Songs and Hymns edited by V. E. Howard; the 1992 Praise for the Lord edited by John P. Wiegand; and the 1999 Into Our Hands edited by Leland R. Fleming; in addition to the 2007 Sacred Songs of the Church edited by William D. Jeffcoat.  It was also used in Stamps-Baxter’s Heavenly Highway Hymns.

The song suggests several reasons why we need to lean on the mighty arm of Jesus.

I. Stanza 1 tells us that we need His protection

“Lean on the mighty arm of Jesus;

Hide in the hollow of His hand.

‘Neath His protecting wings abide you;

Firm on the Rock of Ages stand.”

  1. Jesus will hide us in the hollow of His hand just as God put Moses in the cleft of the rock: Exo. 32:21-22
  2. This protection is also likened to abiding under the shadow of His wings: Ps. 91:4
  3. This will help us to stand firm: Eph. 6:11-14

II. Stanza 2 tells us that we need Him to give us strength and courage

“Lean on the mighty arm of Jesus;
Wait you not for the morning dawn.

Evening of life may come and find you,

And with your strength and courage gone.”

  1. We should not wait for the morning dawn to turn to Jesus because we simply do not know what will happen tomorrow: Jas. 4:14
  2. Evening of life is often used as a symbol to refer to the time of death: Jn. 9:4
  3. Since we do not want to be found with our strength and courage gone, we must watch and pray: Matt. 26:41

III. Stanza 3 tells us that we need His refuge from that which would allure us

“Lean on the mighty arm of Jesus,

For ’tis the only refuge sure;

Let not another’s invitation

Now from this hope your soul allure.”

  1. Jesus is the only refuge sure in the troubles of this world: Ps. 46:1
  2. Therefore, we should not listen to another’s invitation, especially that of the devil who is our adversary and seeks to devour us: 1 Pet. 5:8
  3. Only by leaning on the arm of Jesus can we not be moved away from the hope of the gospel: Col. 1:23

IV. Stanza 4 tells us that we need His boundless mercy

“Lean on the mighty arm of Jesus,

And of His boundless mercy share;

Drink of the ever living fountain

Down by the Rock of Ages there.”

  1. Jesus came to bring us the boundless mercy of God: 1 Tim. 1:13
  2. He leads us to the living fountains of waters: Rev. 7:17
  3. Indeed, He is the Rock from which we drink as did the Israelites in the wilderness: 1 Cor. 10:4

CONCL.:  The chorus reminds us of the importance of trusting in the Lord for courage and guidance.

“Lean on His arm;

Hide in the hollow of His hand!

Lean on His arm;

Firm on the Rock of Ages stand.”

Satan will do everything within His power to shake our faith.  He will use the trials and tribulations of life to cause doubt and will even cast ridicule and persecution in our way to discourage us.  However, we can find strength to press on if we just look to Jesus and “Lean on His Arm.”
